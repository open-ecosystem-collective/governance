# Maturity Levels
 
**Open Ecosystem Collective - 5 Roles, 5 Levels, One Path**
 
The collective has no hierarchy. It has maturity levels.
Like open source projects: Contributor → Committer → Maintainer → Technical Lead → Foundation Member.
 
You grow by doing. Not by waiting. And you grow by lifting others up, not just by climbing.

**License:** CC-BY-SA 4.0

## How to read this document
 
Every Maturity Level has two sides that come together:
 
* **Client-side responsibility** - what you do for paying clients. This is where the day rate sits.
* **Collective-side responsibility** - what you contribute to the community: mentoring, reviewing, maintaining the open methodology, hosting office hours.
You cannot advance through the levels by client work alone. The community-side
contributions are part of every promotion. This is how we keep the collective
alive and how we honour the values: Generosity, Reciprocity, Openness.
 
The model is borrowed in spirit from the Cloud Native Computing Foundation
and Apache Software Foundation: Contributor → Committer → Maintainer →
Technical Lead → Foundation Member. The OEC adapts this for partner
ecosystem work.

## The two parallel paths
 
Every member walks two paths at the same time. They support each other but
they are not the same path.
 
### Path A: Client-side roles
 
This is the work paying clients pay for. Day rates apply. Each level has
defined deliverables and defined scope. This is what is sold as a mandate.
 
### Path B: Collective-side responsibilities
 
This is the work the community needs. Reviewing pull requests in the OEC
repositories. Mentoring newer members. Holding office hours. Maintaining the
methodology so it stays current. None of this is billed.
 
A Maintainer status on a repository is honorific - it carries reputation,
authority, and a badge on your GitHub profile. It does not carry a salary.
That is the open source model and the OEC follows it.
 
A stipend pool for sustained maintainer work may be introduced once the
collective reaches stable mandate volume. Not yet. Reputation first,
remuneration when volume permits.

## Level 1 - Discovery Specialist · Contributor
 
> *The person who opens doors.*
 
### Client-side: what you do
 
You lead the first conversation with a potential client. You map their technology
stack, identify which software vendors they work with, and surface where partner
opportunities exist that they have not yet seen. You do not need to know everything.
You need to be genuinely curious and structured in how you listen.
 
### Client-side: what you deliver
 
**Discovery Report** - covering:
 
* Technology stack and vendor relationships
* Existing partnerships (formal and informal)
* Initial MDF opportunity scan
* Recommended next step (further assessment or direct quick win)
### Client-side: typical engagement
 
1 day on-site or remote · CHF 1'200 · Report delivered within 3 business days
 
### Collective-side: Contributor
 
At Level 1, you are a Contributor in the open-source sense:
 
* You can open issues and submit pull requests in any OEC repository
* Your voice counts in Lazy Consensus decisions (72-hour silence = agreement)
* You attend Roundtables, Discord, and community calls when you can
* You have no formal review or approval rights yet
### Core strengths for this role
 
* Curiosity - asking good questions and actually listening to the answers
* Fast contextual understanding - grasping a new domain quickly
* Structured note-taking - turning conversations into usable information
* Comfort with "I don't know" - and knowing how to find out
### What Level 1 is not
 
Level 1 is not junior. It is specialised. A great Discovery Specialist
opens doors that no one else knew existed. Some people stay at Level 1 by
choice - and that is the right decision if it is where your strongest contribution lies.

## Level 2 - Assessment Lead · Contributor + Reviewer
 
> *The person who maps the territory.*
 
### Client-side: what you do
 
You conduct the Partner Ecosystem Maturity Assessment - the 75-point evaluation
across five dimensions, the structured interview with stakeholders, the scoring,
the gap analysis, and the project mandate recommendation. You translate what you
find into a clear, honest read-out the client can act on. You are comfortable
naming findings the client may not want to hear.
 
The assessment is the gateway to every mandate. No project mandate is scoped
without one. This protects the client from paying for the wrong level of
engagement, and protects the collective from over-promising.
 
### Client-side: what you deliver
 
**Partner Ecosystem Maturity Assessment** covering:
 
* Scores across all 5 dimensions (max 75 points)
* Maturity band classification (Foundation / Emerging / Scaling / Advanced)
* Gap analysis: what is missing for the next level
* Prioritised recommendations
* Project mandate recommendation (tier and roles)
### Client-side: typical engagement
 
Hourly · CHF 175/h · 1-4 hours total based on company size and complexity ·
CHF 175-700 total · Billed separately from any mandate that follows.
 
| Company profile        | Effort | Total      |
| ---------------------- | ------ | ---------- |
| Small (5-8 people, no existing partnerships)     | 1h     | CHF 175    |
| Medium (8-12 people, first partnerships)         | 2h     | CHF 350    |
| Complex (12-20 people, multiple active partners) | 3h     | CHF 525    |
| High complexity (multi-cloud, active MDF)        | 4h     | CHF 700    |
 
The assessment fee is **not** credited toward the project mandate. It is a
standalone service and forms the basis for the proposal that follows.
 
For longer engagements where an Assessment Lead role is embedded in a project
(for example as part of a Foundation or Emerging tier mandate), the daily
rate of CHF 1'400 applies.
 
### Collective-side: Contributor + Reviewer
 
At Level 2, you take on review responsibility in one specific area:
 
* You can give qualified reviews on pull requests in your area of focus
  (e.g. methodology, assessments, governance)
* Your reviews count toward PR approval but you cannot merge alone
* You begin to triage incoming issues, labelling and routing them
* You are expected to attend at least 6 of 12 monthly community calls a year
### Core strengths for this role
 
* Structured analysis - working through a framework systematically
* Communication at C-level - presenting findings to CEO and CTO without losing them
* MDF knowledge - understanding vendor partner programmes in enough detail to identify real opportunities
* Courage in reporting - saying what is true, not what is comfortable
### Prerequisites for advancement to Level 2
 
* At least 3 completed Level 1 Discovery mandates documented
* One peer-reviewed case study published (anonymised)
* Demonstrated familiarity with at least 5 major vendor partner programmes
  (e.g. AWS, Google Cloud, HashiCorp, Red Hat, SUSE, ...)
* **Collective contribution:** at least 3 pull requests merged in any OEC
  repository (typo fixes count, but at least one must be substantive)

## Level 3 - Collaboration Architect · Reviewer + first-time Mentor
 
> *The person who builds bridges between companies.*
 
### Client-side: what you do
 
You design collaboration structures. You identify which companies should work
together, how they should structure a co-sell relationship, and what a realistic
partner roadmap looks like for the next 12 months. You have seen enough deals
to know what makes collaboration work and what makes it fail.
 
### Client-side: what you deliver
 
**Collaboration Plan** - 6-10 pages covering:
 
* Partner shortlist (3-5 specific companies with rationale)
* Co-sell framework for each recommended partnership
* First 90-day action plan
* Template partnership agreement outline
### Client-side: typical engagement
 
3-4 days · CHF 1'600/day · Delivered within 10 business days
 
### Collective-side: Reviewer + first-time Mentor
 
Level 3 is where mentoring becomes mandatory.
 
* You take on at least one Level 1 or Level 2 mentee
* You meet with that mentee at least 2 hours per month
* You document the mentoring relationship in the collective's shared register
* You continue reviewing PRs in your area of focus
* You may be invited to co-chair a small initiative (e.g. a roundtable
  edition, a methodology update sprint)
### Core strengths for this role
 
* Systems thinking - seeing how companies, products, and people connect
* Network understanding - knowing the Cloud Native landscape well enough to spot natural partnerships
* Strategy development - turning observations into structured, actionable plans
* Facilitation - comfortable running conversations between companies who do not yet trust each other
### Prerequisites for advancement to Level 3
 
* At least 5 completed Level 2 Assessment mandates documented
* Two peer-reviewed case studies published
* Direct experience with at least one co-sell or formal partnership negotiation
* Peer review passed by two existing Level 3 or above members
* **Collective contribution:** at least 5 PR reviews completed, with public
  feedback that contributed to the merged version

## Level 4 - Growth Strategist · Maintainer + Mentor
 
> *The person who builds the engine for scale.*
 
### Client-side: what you do
 
You develop multi-year partner growth strategies. You think in OKRs, partner
tier structures, and revenue models. You help clients build the internal
capability to manage partner ecosystems without depending on external support
indefinitely. You understand that a great strategy is one the client can
actually execute.
 
### Client-side: what you deliver
 
**Partner Growth Plan** - 12-18 pages covering:
 
* Partner ecosystem vision for 24 months
* Partner tier structure with entry criteria
* OKR framework for partner team
* MDF roadmap with specific vendors and timelines
* Financial model: expected partner-originated revenue
### Client-side: typical engagement
 
5-6 days · CHF 1'800/day · Delivered within 20 business days
 
### Collective-side: Maintainer + Mentor
 
At Level 4, you carry repository-level responsibility:
 
* **Maintainer status** on one OEC repository (methodology, governance,
  roundtables, landscape, or a future one). You can approve and merge PRs.
* You are listed in `MAINTAINERS.md` of that repository, with your area
  of responsibility and contact
* You hold at least one office hour per quarter (one-hour open slot for
  Contributors and Visitors to ask questions, available on Discord or video)
* You maintain at least one active mentoring relationship at all times
* You may co-chair a Special Interest Group (e.g. SIG-MDF, SIG-CoSell, SIG-ISV)
### Core strengths for this role
 
* Long-term thinking - planning 12-24 months ahead with realistic milestones
* Revenue understanding - partner-originated revenue, MDF ROI, co-sell pipeline tracking
* Structural design - partner tier criteria, programme architecture, governance
* Moderation - facilitating multi-stakeholder strategy sessions
### Prerequisites for advancement to Level 4
 
* At least 5 completed Level 3 mandates documented
* Demonstrated experience building or significantly improving a partner programme
* Three peer-reviewed case studies published
* Peer review passed by two existing Level 4 or above members
* **Collective contribution:**
  * Mentored at least 2 people who have themselves advanced (or stayed
    successfully at Level 1 or 2 by choice) over a sustained 6-month period
  * Hosted at least 2 office hours
  * Made substantial contributions to at least one OEC repository

## Level 5 - Executive Advisor · Steward + Mentor + Maintainer
 
> *The person who thinks alongside the leadership team, and tends to the collective itself.*
 
### Client-side: what you do
 
You operate as a strategic partner to the C-level of a company within a
defined project scope. You provide the framing, the challenge, and the
clarity that leadership teams need when partner decisions have multi-year
consequences. You have the experience and the authority to say what others
in the room will not say.
 
Every Executive Advisor engagement has a clear scope, a defined deliverable,
and an end. The collective does not run open-ended retainers. When a client
needs continuing advisory, a new mandate is scoped.
 
### Client-side: what you deliver
 
**Executive Strategy Engagement** - typically covering:
 
* Partner ecosystem strategic review (one or two stakeholder workshops)
* Strategic recommendations document (4-6 pages)
* Risk and opportunity framing
* Decision support for one or two specific multi-year partner decisions
### Client-side: typical engagement
 
Project-based · CHF 2'400/day · Scope defined per mandate · Typically 4-8 days
delivered over 4-12 weeks · Defined end at scope completion
 
### Collective-side: Steward + Mentor + Maintainer
 
At Level 5, you take responsibility for the collective as a whole:
 
* **Steward status** on cross-cutting concerns: governance changes,
  membership decisions, conflict resolution between Maintainers
* You may hold Maintainer status on multiple repositories
* You attend the Technical Oversight Conversations (quarterly, with all
  Stewards, to set cross-repo direction)
* You actively recruit and mentor potential Level 4 candidates - succession
  planning is part of the role
* You represent the OEC publicly at conferences, in publications, in
  partner programmes (always as one voice among Stewards, never as
  sole representative)
### Core strengths for this role
 
* Executive presence - comfortable in board rooms and investor conversations
* Pattern recognition - having seen enough situations to know which signals matter
* Strategic clarity - cutting through complexity to what actually needs to happen
* Trusted advisor mindset - quality of advice over volume of contact
* Stewardship instinct - tending to the collective as carefully as to clients
### Prerequisites for advancement to Level 5
 
* At least 5 completed Level 4 mandates documented
* Demonstrated C-level client relationship sustained over 12+ months across multiple engagements
* Four peer-reviewed case studies published
* Peer review passed by Tania and at least one existing Level 5 member
* **Collective contribution:**
  * Mentored at least 3 people who have advanced through at least two
    levels of growth
  * Maintained an OEC repository for at least 12 months with documented
    activity
  * Made a recognised contribution to methodology or governance (a major
    framework, a foundational document, a substantial revision)
  * Hosted office hours regularly for at least 12 months

## Mentoring is mandatory, not optional
 
Starting at Level 3, every member must maintain at least one active mentoring
relationship at all times. From Level 4 onward, the requirement is two
relationships at any given moment.
 
### What mentoring looks like
 
* At least 2 hours per month with the mentee (split as the pair sees fit:
  one 2-hour deep-dive, two 1-hour conversations, four 30-minute check-ins)
* A defined focus area chosen by the mentee, supported by the mentor
* A six-month minimum commitment, renewable
* Documented in the collective's shared mentee register (private, only
  visible to Maintainers and Stewards) with start date, focus, and short
  quarterly notes
### Why mandatory
 
A collective that does not transmit its knowledge dies with its founders.
The CNCF and Apache models work because mentoring is a structural
expectation, not an act of grace. The same applies here.
 
If you cannot find someone to mentor, talk to the Stewards. They will pair
you. The default assumption is that there is always at least one person
who could benefit from your level of experience.
 
### What mentoring is not
 
* It is not training. Mentees do the work; mentors give context, feedback,
  and pattern recognition.
* It is not unpaid junior consulting work. Mentees do not work on the
  mentor's client mandates.
* It is not a guarantee of advancement. The mentee still has to do the
  work and meet the prerequisites.
* It is not exclusive. A mentee may have multiple mentors, and a mentor
  may have multiple mentees, within reason (no Steward should mentor more
  than 5 people at once).

## Maintainer responsibilities, in detail
 
Maintainer status on an OEC repository (introduced at Level 4) carries
specific obligations. Without these obligations being met, Maintainer
status is reviewed by Stewards and may be revoked.
 
### What a Maintainer does
 
* **Reviews and merges pull requests** in the repository within a reasonable
  timeframe (target: 7 days for substantive PRs, 48 hours for typo fixes)
* **Triages incoming issues**, labelling them, asking clarifying questions,
  closing duplicates
* **Holds at least one office hour per quarter** specifically for the
  repository (questions, contributor onboarding, design discussions)
* **Drafts the changelog** for any release of the repository
* **Updates the README** when major structural changes happen
* **Identifies and nurtures successor Maintainers** - you should always
  have at least one person you are preparing to take over
### How Maintainer status is granted
 
* A current Maintainer or Steward nominates the candidate
* The candidate must already be Level 4 or above on the client side
* Lazy Consensus (72 hours) applies among existing Maintainers and Stewards
* Once approved, the candidate is added to `MAINTAINERS.md` in the repository
### How Maintainer status ends
 
* **Voluntary stepdown:** you tell the Stewards, you nominate a successor,
  you move to Emeritus status. Honourable and welcomed.
* **Inactivity:** if you do not perform Maintainer duties (no PR reviews,
  no office hours, no triage) for 6 months, Stewards may review the status.
  This is not punitive - life happens, projects shift, priorities change.
  The review is a conversation, not a tribunal.
* **Misalignment with values:** if a Maintainer acts against the values
  charter, Stewards open a discussion. Resolution is sought through
  conversation first. Only in extreme cases is status revoked.

## Lifecycle states
 
A member is in one of these states at any time. State is independent of
Level.
 
* **Active** - currently doing client mandates, fulfilling collective
  responsibilities, available for new work
* **Resting** - taking a planned break (parental leave, sabbatical, other
  obligations). Status preserved. No new mandates assigned. Collective
  responsibilities paused. Minimum 6 months, maximum 24 months without
  re-discussion.
* **Returning** - within the first 3 months back from Resting. A returning
  member shadows one mandate at their previous level before being fully
  reactivated. No re-qualification needed unless a year or more has passed.
* **Emeritus** - formally stepped back from active work. Retains
  consultative voice and Maintainer-emeritus status. Available for occasional
  advisory but not assigned to mandates. Honourable status.
* **Stepped Back** - moved one level down after a peer-review process.
  Rare. Used when a member is consistently underdelivering at their
  current level. Re-advancement possible following the standard process.
Transitions between states are documented. Returning, Stepped Back, and
Emeritus members are listed in the relevant `MAINTAINERS.md` or community
register with their status.

## How progression works
 
Progression is not a promotion. It is a recognition of contribution that
has already happened, on both client and collective sides.
 
```
1. Document your work
   → Client-side: completed mandates in the collective's shared record
   → Collective-side: PRs, reviews, office hours, mentoring records
   → At least one anonymised case study per level published on GitHub
 
2. Peer review
   → Two members at the same level or above review your documented work
   → They check both sides: quality of client delivery, quality of
     collective contribution, adherence to values
 
3. Community vote
   → Lazy Consensus: proposal published to collective channel
   → 72 hours to object. Silence = agreement.
   → Objections require reasoning and an alternative
 
4. Recognition
   → GitHub badge updated
   → MAINTAINERS.md updated if applicable
   → Updated rate tier applies to new mandates from that date
 
5. Give back, immediately
   → Every member who advances commits within 30 days to mentoring
     one person at the level they just left
   → This is non-negotiable. The Generosity value lives here.
```

## Staying at a level by choice
 
There is no obligation to advance. Some people are exceptional at Level 1
and prefer to stay there. That is a legitimate and respected choice. The
collective needs excellent people at every level.
 
Staying at a level does not exempt you from collective-side responsibilities.
A Level 2 by choice still reviews PRs in their area. A Level 3 by choice
still mentors. The mentoring requirement scales with the level you hold,
not with whether you wanted to climb further.
 
Compensation reflects the complexity of the work, not seniority for its own sake.

## What maturity levels are not
 
* They are not a measure of how long you have been in the collective
* They are not a measure of how much you earn (the day rate reflects the
  client value, not your worth as a person)
* They are not permanent - a member who consistently underdelivers may be
  asked to step back to the previous level after a peer review process
* They are not titles to put on a business card without the work behind them
* They are not separable: you cannot be Level 4 on the client side and
  Level 1 on the collective side. The two paths advance together.

## A note on the CNCF inspiration
 
This model owes a lot to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation and the
Apache Software Foundation. Their pattern of Contributor → Committer →
Maintainer is one of the most successful structures the software world
has produced for organising voluntary contribution at scale.
 
The OEC adapts this for a different domain (partner ecosystem management
instead of software development) and a different scale (small collective,
not a global foundation). But the spirit is the same: you earn
responsibility by contributing, you keep it by serving, and you pass it
on by mentoring.
 
Community over Code, applied to a collective of practitioners.
